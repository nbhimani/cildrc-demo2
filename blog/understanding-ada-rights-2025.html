Disability Rights

Understanding Your ADA Rights in 2025

March 15, 2025
5 min read
Debe Fults, Executive Director

The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) continues to be one of the most important civil rights laws protecting individuals with disabilities. As we move through 2025, understanding your rights under the ADA is more crucial than ever.

What is the ADA?

Signed into law in 1990, the ADA prohibits discrimination against individuals with disabilities in all areas of public life, including jobs, schools, transportation, and all public and private places that are open to the general public.

Key Areas of Protection

Employment (Title I)

Employers with 15 or more employees must provide reasonable accommodations to qualified individuals with disabilities, unless doing so would cause undue hardship. This includes:

  • Modified work schedules or job restructuring
  • Accessible facilities and equipment
  • Assistive technology and devices
  • Modified training materials or policies

Public Services (Title II)

State and local government services, programs, and activities must be accessible to people with disabilities. This includes public transportation, voting locations, and government buildings.

Public Accommodations (Title III)

Businesses and nonprofit organizations that serve the public must remove barriers and provide equal access. This includes:

  • Restaurants and retail stores
  • Hotels and theaters
  • Healthcare facilities
  • Gyms and recreation centers

Digital Accessibility

In 2025, digital accessibility has become increasingly important. Websites, mobile apps, and digital services must be accessible to people with disabilities, following WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards.

Recent Updates and Developments

Recent court decisions and regulatory updates have strengthened ADA protections in several areas:

  • Expanded coverage for mental health disabilities
  • Clearer guidelines for website accessibility
  • Stronger protections for service animal access
  • Enhanced requirements for accessible technology in education

Know Your Rights

If you believe your ADA rights have been violated, you have several options:

  1. File a complaint with the appropriate federal agency
  2. File a lawsuit in federal court
  3. Seek assistance from disability rights organizations

How We Can Help

At CILDRC, we provide advocacy support and education about your rights under the ADA. Our team can help you understand your rights, file complaints, and navigate the system to ensure you receive the accommodations and access you deserve.

Contact us today to learn more about our advocacy services and how we can support you in exercising your rights under the ADA.
